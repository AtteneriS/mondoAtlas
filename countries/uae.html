About UAE

The United Arab Emirates is a modern federation of seven unique emirates on the southeastern Arabian Peninsula. It has transformed from historic pearling villages into a hub of global commerce, innovation, and luxury. The UAE combines rich Bedouin heritage with modern skyscrapers like the Burj Khalifa. It serves as a dynamic global melting pot where hospitality traditions meet advanced infrastructure.

Thinking of moving to UAE?

Moving to the UAE comes with no personal income tax, excellent public safety, modern amenities, and a flourishing international business environment. However, relocating here involves dealing with a residency system that depends on sponsors or permits, adapting to extreme summer heat, getting mandatory private health insurance, and respecting Islamic cultural norms along with strict public conduct laws.

Events & Festivals

The UAE's event calendar showcases a blend of Islamic traditions, global entertainment, and national pride. Major religious celebrations like Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha lead to family gatherings and citywide festivities. In addition, high-profile international events like the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix, shopping galas, and food festivals energize the cooler winter months.

All festivals

UAE National Day / Eid Al Etihad

2nd of December

Celebrated on December 2nd, this day honors the 1971 unification of the seven emirates with spectacular fireworks, national parades, cultural performances, and light displays across the country.

Dubai Shopping Festival

Mid-December to Late January

A month-long winter shopping event featuring major retail discounts, drone shows, concerts, daily raffles, and outdoor pop-up markets throughout Dubai.

Eid al-Fitr

Spring / Late Winter (Islamic calendar)

An Islamic celebration marking the end of Ramadan. It is observed with special morning prayers, family feasts, charity, traditional gifts, and festive entertainment across all emirates.

Eid al-Adha

Late Spring / Early Summer (Islamic calendar)

This important Islamic "Feast of Sacrifice" honors Prophet Ibrahim's devotion. It involves family gatherings, community feasts, charity distribution, and extended public holidays.

Abu Dhabi Grand Prix

Late November to Early December

This season-ending Formula 1 race takes place at the Yas Marina Circuit. It features high-speed motorsport, star-studded concerts, and luxury yacht parties.

Dubai Food Festival

Late April to Mid-May

An annual culinary celebration across the city. It showcases Dubai's diverse dining options through street food markets, masterclasses by celebrity chefs, and special menus during restaurant week.

Culture

Emirati culture is based on Islamic values, Bedouin traditions, and Arabian hospitality. This culture is enriched by a global expatriate community that comprises over 85% of the population. Society mixes a modern lifestyle with a strong emphasis on family honor, modesty, religious practices, and polite social behavior in public life.

Values & Customs

Emirati customs focus on generous hospitality, respect for elders, and strong family ties. Guests receive a warm welcome with Arabic coffee (Gahwa) and dates. Cultural values emphasize mutual respect, personal dignity, and social harmony.

Hospitality (Karam) Family Honor Respect

Art & Traditions

Artistic heritage continues through Nabati poetry, detailed Arabic calligraphy, traditional musical dances (Ayyala), and Bedouin crafts such as Al-Sadu weaving. The historical practices of pearl diving, camel racing, and falconry remain cherished national symbols.

Falconry Calligraphy Poetry (Nabati)

Religion

Islam is the official religion and moral foundation of the UAE. It guides laws, daily routines, and holidays. The country promotes religious tolerance and supports churches, Hindu temples, and gurdwaras for its diverse expatriate community.

Islam Places of Worship Call to Prayer

Etiquette

Social etiquette highlights polite greetings, modest clothing in public and government buildings, and using the right hand for eating or passing items. Public displays of affection, offensive language, and filming people without consent are strongly discouraged.

Food

Emirati cuisine is a rich blend of traditional Arabian spices, rice, meats, and seafood, influenced by historical trade routes with India and Persia. Dining in the UAE ranges from age-old Bedouin comfort foods and flavorful street shawarmas to world-class Michelin-starred restaurants representing various international cuisines.

Famous Dishes

Al Harees

A traditional Emirati dish made from cracked wheat and slow-cooked meat or chicken. It is whipped until smooth, topped with clarified butter, and typically served during festivals and Ramadan.

Machboos

A classic spiced rice dish cooked with chicken, lamb, or fish. It is infused with loomi, cardamom, cinnamon, and saffron for a rich, aromatic flavor.

Shawarma

A beloved street food featuring thinly sliced marinated meat, roasted on a vertical spit. It is wrapped in flatbread with garlic sauce, pickles, and fries.

Luqaimat

A popular traditional dessert made of golden, crispy deep-fried dough balls. They are drizzled with date syrup and sprinkled with sesame seeds.

Balaleet

A unique breakfast dish that combines sweet and savory flavors. It consists of sweetened vermicelli noodles seasoned with cardamom, saffron, and rose water, topped with a savory egg omelet.

Samboosa

Crispy fried triangular pastries filled with spiced vegetables, minced meat, or cheese. These are popular savory appetizers or snacks during Ramadan.

Knafeh

A decadent Middle Eastern pastry made from spun shredded dough, layered with melted cheese. It is soaked in fragrant sugar syrup and topped with pistachios.

Camel Milk

A traditional Bedouin drink, rich in nutrients and lower in fat than cow's milk. It can be enjoyed plain, flavored, or infused into local chocolates and lattes.

Weather

The UAE has an arid desert climate with two main seasons: a mild, sunny winter and a very hot summer. Winters, from November to April, provide comfortable temperatures suitable for outdoor activities. Summers, from May to September, bring extreme temperatures over 40°C, along with high humidity along the coast.

To be mindful of

Best Time

November to March

This period offers pleasant temperatures between 20 and 28°C, low humidity, and clear skies. It is the best time to explore neighborhoods, attend business networking events, tour housing options, and enjoy lively outdoor street life and cultural festivals comfortably.

Be wary: Extreme Summer Heatwave and Humidity

June to September

Temperatures often go over 42°C, along with high coastal humidity, making outdoor activities unsafe. Many businesses slow down, expatriates travel abroad for vacation, and scouting neighborhoods outdoors becomes difficult without staying inside air-conditioned spaces.

Be wary: Holy Month of Ramadan

Approx. February to March (Islamic Calendar)

During Ramadan, public eating, drinking, and smoking are not allowed during fasting hours. Business hours in both public and private sectors are shorter, administrative processes slow down, and commercial activity shifts mostly to the evening.

Landmarks

The UAE's landscape features famous architectural landmarks and natural desert views. Highlights include Abu Dhabi's stunning Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque and Louvre Museum, along with Dubai's impressive Burj Khalifa, the man-made Palm Jumeirah, and the ancient mountain roads of Ras Al Khaimah. The country presents a striking contrast between its desert heritage and ambitious architecture.

Burj Khalifa

Dubai

Soaring 828 meters over Downtown Dubai, this building is the world's tallest and symbolizes the country's rapid rise. For newcomers, it anchors a lively neighborhood filled with offices, fine dining, and residential towers.

Sheikh Zayed Grand Mosque

Abu Dhabi

Located in Abu Dhabi, this stunning white-marble mosque is the primary place of worship in the nation. It welcomes all cultures and offers a calm view into Islamic heritage, traditional craftsmanship, and local architectural pride.

Palm Jumeirah

Dubai

A world-famous, palm-shaped artificial island extending into the Arabian Gulf. It showcases the UAE's engineering ambitions and offers a peaceful waterfront lifestyle with upscale resorts, luxury apartments, scenic boardwalks, and popular dining spots.

Dubai Marina

Dubai

A vibrant waterfront district with canal-style lines of skyscrapers and a 7-kilometer promenade. It is popular among expatriates and features a lively neighborhood with easy access to public transport, beaches, cafes, and outdoor fitness areas.

Louvre Abu Dhabi

Abu Dhabi

A beautiful universal museum located beneath a striking domed canopy on Saadiyat Island. It highlights the UAE's role as a cultural bridge, showcasing shared human history through art collections from ancient to modern times.

Hatta

Dubai Emirate

An inland mountain area nestled in the Hajar range, offering a stark contrast to coastal skylines. It serves as a popular weekend getaway for residents looking for outdoor adventures, mountain biking, kayaking across turquoise reservoirs, and experiencing heritage farming culture.

Immigration

The UAE provides modern, efficient visa options aimed at attracting foreign talent, investors, entrepreneurs, and retirees. Recent changes in legislation have introduced long-term self-sponsored options, including the 10-year Golden Visa, 5-year Green Visa, and Remote Work Visa. These changes lessen the past reliance on local employer sponsorship and make long-term residency much easier.

Department of immigration

Immigration in the UAE functions through a federal and local system. The main federal authority is the ICP (Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Port Security). This agency manages national identity cards (Emirates ID), entry permits, and federal visa rules. In Dubai, the GDRFA (General Directorate of Residency and Foreigners Affairs) specifically handles immigration matters.
